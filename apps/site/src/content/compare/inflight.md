MarkLayer and [Inflight](https://inflight.co) both put a comment layer over a page so reviewers can point instead of describe, and both hand that feedback to an AI coding agent over MCP. They differ on what the page is. Inflight is built for prototypes: a script tag goes into the preview build, each build becomes a version, reviewers leave pinned comments, video replies and poll answers, and the feedback rolls up into next steps for an agent to complete. MarkLayer is built for any page that already exists: paste the URL at marklayer.app and the annotation layer loads over the live site with nothing deployed, or open the free browser extension on localhost or a page behind a login.

The second difference is who pays. Inflight charges $15 to $18 per member a month, with reviewers free. MarkLayer is free with no seats, and neither the reviewer nor the person who starts the review needs an account.

Inflight has a real lead on structured review. Video replies arrive with transcripts, polls collect answers rather than opinions, Slack threads sync both ways, and every build is kept with its own password. MarkLayer's video notes have no transcript, it has no polls, and it posts to Slack one way. What MarkLayer has instead is the room: live cursors, a peer-to-peer voice or video call on the page, drawing and measuring tools, an inspector that builds a prompt-ready block for an agent, and an MCP server on every link that can resolve threads and leave its own suggestions. If the review is "look at this build with me and vote", pick Inflight. If it is "circle what is wrong on this page and let the agent fix it", pick MarkLayer.
